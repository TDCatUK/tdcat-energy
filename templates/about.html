The Project

This dashboard is a passion project built and maintained by Torsten Dettlaff. It was created to bridge the massive gap between personal home energy systems and the volatile realities of the UK's macro-grid.

Why did I build it? Because I wanted to understand more about energy data and the infrastructure and markets behind it.

Local Weather & Analytics

Weather data and 7-day forecasts are fetched via the Open-Meteo API. Site analytics are pulled from the Cloudflare GraphQL API.

  • Daylight Length & Irradiance: Rather than just showing when the sun is up, the forecast charts track Direct Normal Irradiance (W/m²). This measures the amount of solar radiation actually reaching the surface, providing a highly accurate leading indicator for expected home solar generation.
  • Temp, Wind, & Rain: Core meteorological data used to contextualize macro-grid demand (e.g., cold days driving up gas heating demand) and national wind generation forecasts.
  • Site Visitors (Header): The small user icon in the top navigation bar displays the rolling 24-hour unique visitor count for this dashboard, queried directly from Cloudflare's edge network.

Home Metrics (Tesla Powerwall)

Data is scraped directly from the local IP address of a residential Powerwall 2 gateway every 60 seconds, bypassing the cloud entirely to ensure zero latency.

  • Home Load: The real-time electrical draw of the entire house. Includes all active appliances, lighting, and parasitic drains.
  • Solar Generating (AC vs DC Est): The main figure represents the real-time AC (Alternating Current) power successfully inverted and available to your home or the grid. The smaller DC Est figure represents the raw DC (Direct Current) power generated by the solar panels on the roof. The discrepancy between these two numbers is due to physical system losses—primarily the energy lost as heat during the DC-to-AC conversion process within the inverter, along with minor electrical resistance in the cabling.
  • Battery Level: The current State of Charge (SoC) of the Powerwall. The accompanying text indicates the precise flow (Discharging to support the house/grid, or Charging from excess solar/grid).
  • Grid Flow: The real-time electrical exchange with the national grid. "Importing" indicates buying power to meet home demand or charge the battery. "Exporting" indicates selling excess solar or battery power back to the network.
  • Trapezoidal Integration (Cumulative kWh): Because live power is measured in instantaneous Watts, calculating the daily accumulated kWh (kilowatt-hours) requires complex math. The backend calculates the precise time delta down to the second between every database row, utilizing Trapezoidal Integration to map the exact geometric area under the curve.

Octopus Energy

Rates are pulled live via the Octopus Developer API. Consumption data is reconciled via the DCC (Data Communications Company) smart meter network.

  • Live Import Rate: The exact price being charged (in pence per kWh) to pull electricity from the grid at this very second. Dynamically tracks Time of Use (TOU) tariffs like Agile and Flux.
  • Live Export Rate: The exact price being paid (in pence per kWh) to push solar or battery energy back to the grid.
  • Elec Import/Export (Official kWh): Because the national smart meter network batches data, this represents the exact, billable energy exchanged with the grid during the most recent 24-hour settlement period available.
  • Gas Used (Official kWh): UK SMETS2 gas meters natively record volume in cubic meters (m³), not energy. This metric automatically applies the standard UK conversion formula (m³ × ~11.222) to convert raw gas flow into billable thermal kWh.

National Grid Metrics

Macro-grid data is sourced from the Elexon BMRS (Balancing Mechanism Reporting Service), the National Grid ESO Carbon API, and Sheffield Solar.

  • Grid Demand: The total gigawatts (GW) of electrical power currently being consumed across the United Kingdom.
  • Generation: The total GW of electrical power being actively generated by all metered power stations and scaled solar estimates.
  • Net Flow: The total physical flow of electricity moving through the underwater interconnectors connecting the UK to France, Ireland, Belgium, the Netherlands, Denmark, and Norway. An "Import" state (down arrow) means the UK is buying power; an "Export" state (up arrow) means the UK is selling power.
  • Frequency (Hz): The real-time alternating current frequency of the grid. It must be maintained extremely close to 50.000 Hz to prevent catastrophic hardware failures.
  • Carbon Intensity (gCO₂): The environmental impact of generating one kilowatt-hour of electricity, measured in grams of carbon dioxide. Heavily influenced by the ratio of wind/solar generation to gas/coal generation.

Wholesale Markets & Imbalance

The UK energy market operates on a system of forecasting and real-time correction. The prices and metrics you see reflect this balancing act:

  • Market Index Price (MIDP): Reflects short-term wholesale prices (often tied to the EPEX SPOT intraday markets). This gives a baseline indication of what energy is trading for on the open market right now, before any emergency balancing actions are taken.
  • System Imbalance (NIV): The Net Imbalance Volume (NIV) represents the physical megawatt-hour (MWh) deficit or surplus on the grid at any given moment. A positive (short) number means the grid is demanding more power than is being generated, forcing the grid to buy expensive emergency power. A negative (long) number means there is too much power, forcing the grid to pay generators to turn off.
  • Balancing Price (System Sell Price): Because grid forecasting is never perfect, National Grid relies on a real-time auction to force generators to correct the NIV and keep the grid at 50Hz. This price fluctuates wildly and acts as a real-time financial penalty to energy companies who fail to accurately forecast their customers' usage.

The 4 Levels of Demand

National Grid accounting separates "Demand" into four specific layers based on exactly what the grid has to service.

1. National Demand (INDO)

This is the purest baseline. It represents the actual metered electricity requested strictly by end-consumers connected to the transmission network. It completely ignores interconnectors pushing power out to Europe, power stations using electricity themselves, or hydro stations acting as giant batteries.

2. Transmission System Demand (ITSDO)

This is the burden the physical grid actually has to handle. It takes National Demand and adds all the heavy industrial lifting the grid has to supply. Mathematically, it is INDO plus:

  • Exports: Electricity being pumped to France, Belgium, Norway, etc.
  • Pumped Storage Pumping: Gigawatts of power used to push water uphill into reservoirs (like Dinorwig) to act as grid-scale batteries.
  • Station Load: The immense "parasitic" electricity draw required just to run the power stations themselves. Elexon natively hardcodes this as a permanent 500 MW (0.5 GW) constant.

3. Actual Demand Net

This is what households and businesses are actively consuming. It takes National Demand and adds back Embedded Generation. Because rooftop solar and smaller local wind turbines (LV Wind) sit "behind the meter," the National Grid can't see them—it just sees national demand drop when the sun comes out or the wind blows locally. This figure adds algorithmic solar and LV wind estimates back in to show true societal energy usage.

4. Actual Demand Gross

This is the absolute maximum amount of electricity moving through the entire UK ecosystem. It combines the heavy physical load of the Transmission System Demand (including exports and pumping) and adds the localized Embedded Generation (Solar and LV Wind) on top.

Generation Fuel Types & The Dashed Line

The solid stacked area on the charts represents all power being pushed into the grid, while the dashed line represents power being pulled out.

What is the White Dashed Line?

The dashed line overlaid on the 24-Hour Generation chart represents total Grid Demand. When total generation (the solid colors) spikes above this dashed line, it means the grid is over-producing. This excess, zero-carbon energy is actively soaked up by grid-scale batteries or used to pump water uphill at hydro stations to store the power for the evening peak.

  • HV Wind: Total generation from large, transmission-connected onshore and offshore wind farms across the UK. Often the primary source of clean energy.
  • LV Wind (Embedded): Estimated generation from smaller wind turbines connected to the local distribution network. Like rooftop solar, the grid cannot "see" this generation directly, so it is mathematically modelled by the National Energy System Operator (NESO).
  • Solar: Estimated national solar generation, calculated by scaling up sample data from thousands of monitored PV systems.
  • Nuclear: Baseload power provided by the UK's active nuclear reactor fleet. Extremely low carbon but highly inflexible.
  • Gas (CCGT): Combined Cycle Gas Turbines. High-efficiency power stations that use both a gas and a steam turbine together.
  • Gas (OCG): Open Cycle Gas Turbines. Fast-starting but inefficient peaker plants used strictly to cover sudden peaks in national demand.
  • Hydro & Pumped Storage: Traditional hydro-electric generation. Pumped Storage utilizes reservoirs that act as a massive national battery.
  • Biomass: Power stations (like Drax) that burn organic matter. Officially categorized as renewable, though highly debated.
  • Other: Miscellaneous generation sources absorbing trace outputs from decommissioned networks.
